### What the ASJC codes tell you about scope — and it matters

This is the single most useful thing the Scopus record gives us, and it cuts
against the stated plan.

**TTS carries exactly one subject code: 2201, Engineering (miscellaneous).** It
holds *no* health, medical or informatics classification at all. Scopus does not
treat TTS as a health-informatics venue, and neither do its reviewers. A paper
submitted there has to earn its place on the *technology-and-society* argument —
what offline-first design does for health equity in a village — not on the
engineering of the platform.

**JBHI carries four codes, two of them squarely health informatics (3605, 2718).**
It is also Medline-sourced, which means acceptance there additionally buys PubMed
discoverability. It is the better *subject* fit and the worse *budget* fit, as
Section 2 shows.

### Caveats stated honestly

- Coverage "2020–2026" and "2013–2026" are the ranges in the October 2026 list.
  Both show as Active, so coverage is ongoing, not closed.
- `scopus.com/sources` requires an Elsevier login and was not accessed.
  The downloadable official list is equivalent in authority for the fields above.
- No CiteScore, SNIP or SJR figure is asserted anywhere in this document,
  because none was obtained from a primary source. **Do not put an impact factor
  or CiteScore in the manuscript or the cover letter.** If you want one, read it
  off the journal's own Xplore page yourself and cite that page with the date.
- **Scopus indexing of your article is not instantaneous.** Both journals having
  active coverage means articles they publish are indexed in due course, not that
  your paper appears in Scopus on the day of publication. Expect a lag measured
  in weeks to months after Xplore publication.

## 3. Preprint status — a real risk at JBHI, probably fine at TTS

Your preprint is live: **Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22879440**.

**JBHI's editorial policy is the problem.** It states that to qualify for
publication, manuscripts must be "previously unpublished and NOT under
consideration elsewhere (including journals, conferences **or any form of online
publication**)." The cover letter must confirm the work "has not been
copyrighted, published, submitted, or accepted for publication elsewhere."

A Zenodo deposit with its own DOI is, on a literal reading, a form of online
publication. The pages read contain **no** preprint-friendly carve-out and **no**
stated disclosure procedure. The only preprint sentence found concerns Xplore
compliance of author-created PDFs, which is a different matter.

- **Status: UNVERIFIED and genuinely risky.**
- **Action required:** email the JBHI Editorial Office *before* submitting, state
  the DOI, and get a written answer. Do this only if you decide to target JBHI.

**TTS's author pages say nothing about preprints at all.** Status: **UNVERIFIED**.
IEEE's organisation-wide policy generally permits preprint posting, but that was
not confirmed from a primary source in this check, so it is not asserted here.
Confirm with the TTS editorial office or the IEEE Author Center before
submission.

**What you must not do, and your brief already says so:** do not delete, hide or
quietly retract the Zenodo deposit to get around a journal policy. Zenodo DOIs
are permanent and the deposit is discoverable; concealing it would be a
publication-ethics violation far more damaging than a desk rejection. Disclose it
in the cover letter either way.

One upside worth noting: the preprint establishes your priority date on the
GramCare AI design. Nobody can scoop the architecture while you spend the next
weeks generating evidence.

## 4. Submission portals — conflicting information, resolve before you start

Both journals' own pages disagree with themselves about which system is live.

| Journal | Portal named on the "prepare/submit" page | Portal named elsewhere on the same site |
|---|---|---|
| TTS | IEEE Author Portal — `https://ieee.atyponrex.com/journal/TTS` | the open-access section refers to "IEEE Author Gateway in Manuscript Central" |
| JBHI | IEEE Author Portal — `https://ieee.atyponrex.com/journal/jbhi-embs` | editorial policy and FAQ say "ONLY through ScholarOne" — `http://mc.manuscriptcentral.com/jbhi-embs` |

IEEE has been migrating titles from ScholarOne to the Atypon-based Author Portal,
which is the likely explanation. **Open the Author Portal link for your chosen
journal first and see whether it accepts a new submission for that title.** Do
not start preparing portal-specific files until you know which system you are in.

### Format constraint that affects your tooling

**TTS requires a `.DOC` template and explicitly does not accept LaTeX.** The
template is linked from its author page. If you target TTS, write in Word, not
LaTeX — plan for that now, because it changes how figures, tables and equations
have to be produced.
